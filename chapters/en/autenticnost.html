Being yourself is not an easy path, but it is the only path that leads to true happiness and inner peace.

Being yourself seems like the most natural and simplest thing in the world. Yet being authentic becomes an insurmountable challenge for many.

When a person refuses to be themselves, they fall into a whirlwind of endless inner conflicts. Examples of pretense or self-deception, when a person plays someone they are not in reality, we can observe around us on a daily basis. It is a way to escape from painful reality. People commonly hide their true face and actual existence not only from the world but also from themselves. However, if the outer expression of an individual diverges from their inner world, it leads to stress, anxiety, guilt, or even shame.

We can cite several such examples:

an individual who advises others on financial strategies while struggling with financial lack themselves;

someone who explains at every turn the importance of a healthy lifestyle, yet constantly faces health problems;

a person who declares that social media is a waste of time, yet privately spends entire hours scrolling through its content daily;

a person who presents spiritual values and personal development, yet paradoxically participates in various parties where they drink alcohol, establish superficial acquaintances, or even take drugs, activities in direct contrast to spiritual values and sustained personal growth;

a person who advises others how to eat, yet their attempts at self-control regularly fail and overeating is a common practice for them.

These are only some examples of how people can be inauthentic and how they create images of themselves that do not correspond to reality at all. The lack of authenticity and self-reflection not only weakens their own position but also diminishes their credibility and respect from others.

People tend to be sincere with themselves only rarely. If they were sincere with themselves, they would have to acknowledge their weaknesses and face their own imperfections. However, this requires great courage and inner strength. From the perspective of a fundamental biological principle, it also requires a great deal of energy, which our "Primitive Self" naturally tries to prevent. Recognition and correction of deficiencies require change in thinking, change in behavior, and corrective steps. Every change requires effort and this effort consumes energy. Let us recall that a living organism craves energy and subconsciously always chooses the path of least resistance. In other words, at the most basic level, at every moment we try to increase our energy, waste it less, or spend it efficiently. The "Primitive Self" will therefore never seek out or prioritize sincerity, truthfulness, or authenticity. One could look at it from the opposite side as well—if we are not sincere, truthful, and authentic, certain aspects of the "Primitive Self" probably still dominate within us.

Another reason why people are not sincere with themselves is often fear of rejection or misunderstanding by society. We simply fear that when we show our true face and reveal our weaknesses, we will become targets of criticism or ridicule. At the most primitive level, we fear exclusion from the group, which in the past meant death, since our survival in harsh nature depended on other members of the group. So we prefer to remain what is expected of us. Or we are what we think we should be—our idealized image, which we have created in our heads. Although it does not correspond to reality, a person tends to use it as a form of self-defense mechanism. Creating a positive image of ourselves is even a way for an individual to increase their self-esteem.

Accepting the truth about ourselves, especially if it is subjectively perceived as negative, requires emotional strength and courage, which is too demanding for most people. Complete acceptance of responsibility for one's life is unimaginable for a large part of the population. Research on consciousness shows that people tell themselves the truth in only about seven percent of cases. This fact points to how often we prefer self-deception to self-reflection, sincerity, and truth.

In the context of evolution, this makes sense. Remaining in self-deceptions, pretenses, and illusions is a simpler solution from the perspective of energy efficiency because it does not require change. It is even supported by various defensive psychological mechanisms of our "Primitive Self" and "Rational Self." From a short-term perspective, this illusion provides certain false psychological rewards, but in reality it is a long-term source of stress, inner conflict, and leads to long-term stagnation and failure. A person simply feels deeply within the inconsistencies between their deeds and words, which creates inner tension, stress, diminished self-esteem, and also diminished trust and respect from others. An individual living in self-deception easily becomes untrustworthy and repellent, while constantly wondering why they are not succeeding.

The discrepancy between the real state and the idealized image resembles the properties of lies, which in communication with other people tends to result in various nonverbal physical manifestations—for example, accelerated heart rate, sweating, nervousness, frequent blinking, avoiding eye contact, or conversely overly direct eye contact. They are described in detail in various books and psychological articles on nonverbal communication, yet people often can recognize them without being experts in the field. The higher the level of consciousness of an individual, the more sensitive they are to nonverbal signals. The reason is that an individual with a high level of consciousness has better developed empathy, compassion, and has a strong ability of intuitive understanding, which provides them with countless evolutionary advantages. People with higher consciousness are naturally attuned to perceiving subtle signals because they are internally focused on truthfulness, authenticity, and the depth of human relationships, which causes them to be far more sensitive to inconsistencies in communication.

The emotional storm that inner conflict evokes has not only psychological but also tangible physical consequences. This phenomenon, known as cognitive dissonance, deeply influences behavior, health, and interactions with the world. The split between who we are and how we present ourselves is more than just a psychological concept—it is a struggle for our authenticity. It is a struggle for our "true self."

If we are truly authentic and live according to what we say, people will naturally recognize this without the necessity of words. Authenticity manifests through deeds, not words. For example, a true entrepreneur does not need to constantly emphasize that they are an entrepreneur, because their actions, way of thinking, their decisions, and approach to work point to it without the necessity of words. It is evident from the way they solve problems, how they lead their team, how they negotiate the best conditions, or how they innovate and adapt to the market situation. Such a person does not require confirmation of their identity from others because their reality and self-consciousness are anchored in their deeds, results, and recurring successes. It is about a natural expression of being that does not need ornamental descriptions or persuasion. Their truthfulness is apparent to everyone willing to see it.

The opposite example would be someone who tries to pretend their entrepreneurial abilities without real results or experience. Such an individual tends to frequently speak of "magnificent" entrepreneurial plans and advise others on what is right and wrong, yet in reality does not run any successful business and their "enterprises" exist nowhere but in their imagination. Individuals who portray themselves as entrepreneurs, yet their actions and abilities do not actually correspond to this image, commonly occur in various companies at various positions. Through their presentation and shared information they try to create the impression of experienced businessmen, although they often lack tangible proof of their successes. Their conduct does not reflect actual business wisdom at all, but rather an immature and naive perspective without a real foundation. Despite being incapable of making strategic decisions, bearing responsibility for their actions, and usually leaving the resolution of serious problems to others, they imagine themselves as experienced entrepreneurs who have the right to advise others. Their distorted self-image does not change even by the fact that their financial compensation does not come from entrepreneurial activity but from positions in management, creative, or administrative fields. Business owners regularly encounter many similar examples.

The discrepancy between an illusory self-image and actual abilities leads only to inner conflicts, frustration, negative emotions, and repeated failure. People are intuitively sensitive to contradictions between words and deeds, so these illusions and self-deceptions do not remain hidden for long. Being authentic and truthful ultimately helps us not only avoid stress and unpleasant emotional states such as guilt or shame, but also helps us connect more deeply with others. When we are authentic and sincere, other people feel it, they trust us more and respect us more. In this way we manage to build stronger and more meaningful personal and professional relationships, which leads to greater harmony, satisfaction, happiness, peace, and love as well as professional success.

With the development of the level of consciousness comes fundamental knowledge—being authentic represents the only path to true happiness and inner harmony. Our "Higher Self" naturally prefers truthfulness, authenticity, and sincerity over pretense, masks, and fulfilling the expectations of others. Whether it is communication directed toward us or away from us.

Advanced consciousness equally well realizes that fulfilling the expectations of others and playing theater for them causes only discomfort, stress, disappointment, and inner disharmony. Being yourself is far more important for our happiness and inner peace than impressing someone with a false mask and pretense. Furthermore, such theater always leads to feelings of guilt and shame, which are low emotional states with strongly negative effects on life.

The higher the level of consciousness we have, the more we refuse to do anything against ourselves. If the "Higher Self" prevails, we can no longer be an insincere, untruthful, and inauthentic version of ourselves. We realize that a constant effort to fulfill others' expectations will always lead us only to inner emptiness, inner conflicts, and psychological and health problems. The discrepancy between our emotional and cognitive aspects is very exhausting and has destructive effects on our physical health.

Conversely, being truly who we are opens doors to true satisfaction and inner harmony. In this context, authenticity becomes not only a path to personal happiness and satisfaction, but also a fundamental pillar of our mental, emotional, and physical well-being. Understanding and accepting this concept is not simple because it requires an honest look into ourselves, deep self-reflection, and willingness to face our deepest fears and uncertainties. The reward for the courage to be authentic and live a life in accord with truth is, however, invaluable.

With the shift in consciousness comes deep realization that being authentic is not only the only path to true happiness and harmony, but also a responsibility toward ourselves and the surrounding world. When we are authentic, when we are sincere, when we speak truth, when we firmly hold to our values, we become an example for others and inspire them toward greater self-esteem, self-love, and self-acceptance, thereby contributing to positive change in our entire surroundings. An authentic life is therefore not only a path to personal satisfaction but also a catalyst for broader social transformation.
