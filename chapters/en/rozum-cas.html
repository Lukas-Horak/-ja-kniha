Living in the present allows us to savor each moment, without constantly seeking something more or something that is to come later.

Time helps us structure and organize our daily activities. In interaction with other people or when performing routine life activities, it usually plays a significant role. The ability to perceive time helped individuals in the animal world increase their chances of survival because it allowed them to better perceive their environment, remember potential threats, and predict the movement of predators or prey.

After our ancestors realized the repeating movement of the Sun, the motion of oceans, or our planet, they began recording time. It was the rotation of Earth that became the basis for measuring time.

Through time we can measure and classify various events, compare their durations, and determine the speed of changes. We are also governed by time when planning work, meals, sleep, and performing daily duties.

Our collective ability to perceive time is also closely connected to the concept of intersubjectivity. Through intersubjectivity, the ability of multiple individuals to agree on certain ideas, we can create and maintain abstract concepts such as state borders, the value of money, or legal laws. These concepts exist primarily as a product of human minds and are maintained by collective agreement. Intersubjectivity also allows us to work on common goals. Thanks to our ability to imagine future outcomes, we can combine forces and work on realizing (so far) immaterial futures, whether it is the construction of a skyscraper, an innovative technology, or the discovery of a cure. Many discoveries and innovations came about thanks to an imaginary idea of a specific future.

However, we would have difficulty explaining to an ape that if it stacks bricks all day, we will give it bananas in the evening. Apes, like other animals, have limited cognitive abilities and are primarily driven by immediate needs and stimuli. They do not have a developed ability to understand abstract concepts such as time or future events. Animals live in the present moment. We humans assume that our perception of time is actual reality. However, this is not true. The universe does not care how people on the planet Earth perceive time.

Our perception of time is largely the result of biological and psychological processes that allow us to function efficiently in the physical world. The universe has no preferred perception of time. According to the theory of relativity developed by Albert Einstein, time and space blend and create a four-dimensional continuum called spacetime. Within spacetime, time can move at different speeds depending on the strength of gravity or the speed of movement. This means that what we perceive as the same moment in time may actually be a different time for another observer under different conditions.

Albert Einstein once wrote: "People like us who believe in physics know that the difference between past, present, and future is only a stubborn illusion." Some physicists have aligned with Einstein and agree that true reality is timeless or atemporal. This same view of reality is also part of many spiritual teachings. At the deepest level of existence, everything is interconnected and unified. Concepts like time and space are merely relative and limited ways that the human brain interprets and organizes experiences.

Time is very useful for practical aspects of life in the material world. In the context of consciousness development, however, time is perceived only as a tool of mind that helps sequentially organize our experiences. It has no absolute or permanent reality. Essentially, it is a construct that serves to structure and understand the sequence of events; however, the higher the level of consciousness we have, the less important time perception is to us. In the highest states of consciousness, time loses its dominant role and becomes less relevant. Past and future are seen only as illusions bound to personal identity and were necessary for survival in harsh nature.

A large amount of human suffering is connected precisely to the ability to perceive time the way we perceive it. Most animals live in the present moment. They do not worry about what might happen in five months, nor about what happened six years ago. If we focused more on the present moment, many of our problems and psychological suffering would disappear. If we stop focusing on past mistakes, unpleasant experiences, and stop letting the present moment be spoiled by what cannot be changed, enormous liberation will come. The same applies to ideas about the potential future, which usually lead to fear and similarly prevent us from fully savoring the present.

It is our mind that creates worries about the future or regret about the past. Despite the fact that we cannot predict the exact future and cannot change the past, we constantly cause ourselves suffering through various negative emotional states. Without our permission, our own intellect creates them, which constantly evaluates, judges, criticizes, comments, worries, assumes, and regrets.

If we are stuck in the past, it is not possible to move forward. Some people build their entire identity around their past experiences. Their narcissistic ego enjoys having experienced some injustice, and they repeatedly emphasize how they suffered. I suffered. I was wronged. I paid the price for that. No one else! Me, me, me.

They voluntarily remain stuck in moments they could have let go of years or decades ago. Their ego enjoys small false psychological rewards in the form of self-pity, a sense of grievance, moral victory, shame, guilt, or playing the victim.

If we are stuck in the past because of our own failures, it is also only a narcissistic ego that pities itself, feels shame, guilt, or other negative emotions. It constantly points to itself and its mistakes, perceived as the center of all suffering. I made a mistake. I failed. My mistake is the worst of all. Me, me, me.

The "Primitive Self" can distort our perspective on reality and leave us trapped in a cycle of negative emotions. This state of mind ignores the broader context and higher awareness in which we consider mistakes and failures as an integral part of human growth and development. Instead of drowning in self-pity and endless cycles of guilt, we should realize that every mistake and failure is an opportunity to learn, grow, and develop. The process of increasing consciousness requires that we also transcend such limitations of our "Primitive Self" and "Rational Self."

The perspective of the "Higher Self" allows us to perceive mistakes not as definitive failures but as steps on the path to deeper self-understanding or life in general. Higher consciousness frees us from the need to be perfect in every moment and allows us to accept ourselves with love and compassion. We should also realize that what we once considered reality was probably only a distorted image of our "Primitive Self." Not only do we not remember events precisely, the old version of ourselves perceived them. The memories we carry are not an objective record of reality but our interpretation stemming from our state of consciousness. In other words, the past reflects our state of consciousness at that time more than reality itself. This is why it is important not to become stuck in the past but to move on. No thought about reality is reality.

It is similar to when we look back at an old argument with our partner. We were then convinced we were completely right, but with distance we see that we reacted from fear, anger, or another low emotional state. The situation was not as we perceived it then but was a reflection of our state of mind at that time. There is no point in becoming stuck in any moment from the past because that moment no longer exists. All that remains is our memory of it, which is moreover distorted by ego and the state of consciousness we were in at that time.

Higher consciousness allows us to forgive not only others but also ourselves. We do not consider our past mistakes as a reason for shame, self-pity, or guilt but understand them as important lessons that help us grow. We realize that our mistakes are not the center of the universe nor do they define our overall worth. When we manage to transcend this egocentric view of perception, we can experience a greater spectrum of emotions including self-love, self-esteem, and empathy. This process allows us to overcome old patterns of thinking and move from evolutionarily older emotional states to higher states of consciousness.

The reality is that we bear full responsibility for our lives. Only we decide how we will respond to various life situations. We can choose to forgive ourselves and others. We can choose to approach events with full understanding, compassion, and love, or conversely, hold grudges and remain stuck in low emotional states. Narcissistic regret or blaming ourselves or others, as mentioned, brings people psychological pleasure. In the short term this may seem harmless or even useful, but in the long term we create obstacles for our personal growth and development.

Mature and conscious people can take full responsibility for their lives, understand, learn, and move on. They do not let the past spoil their days, which cannot be changed. People with low consciousness, on the other hand, continue to be angry like small children, feel shame, drown in guilt, blame themselves and others, point fingers, complain, and pity themselves. For some, the endless game of victim and perpetrator becomes a way to maintain significance and value in the social hierarchy, even though it actually has a destructive effect on life. If we notice even the slightest traces of this in ourselves, it is necessary to end it. That we played the victim is no longer significant. It is okay. This game arose unconsciously as a defense mechanism. We must forgive that too to free ourselves from this role.

If, conversely, we live in the future, we constantly fear it, imagine various irrational scenarios, or constantly hope for something better than what we have now. If our future projections are negative, we feel fear, worry, and stress. If they are positive, we expect something in the future to reduce our suffering or bring us happiness. We imagine how a larger house, a nicer car, higher income, a longer vacation, a new wardrobe, a luxury handbag, a different relationship, or a different life situation will ensure lasting contentment. Then I will be happy. If these imaginations do not materialize, we suffer.

The cruel truth is that people suffer equally even when their imaginations come true. They are disappointed when they realize that fulfilling their dream did not bring the happiness they dreamed of. Years they chase certain milestones, and when they reach them, nothing fundamentally changes. The moment they reach their goal, they begin chasing the next one.

Let us recall the primitive and insatiable cycle of desire and reward from the first part of the book that governs the lives of millions of people. In most cases, it is just a small dose of dopamine that gives people pleasant feelings for a moment. After a short time, however, comes shock. They achieved what they so desired, and everything is the same despite that. It is great disappointment. A person feels that the entire world has deceived them. They sacrificed years of life, saved money, deferred enjoyment of the present moment—and nothing.

Yes, it is a fraud. It has always been fraud. No final point exists in the physical world. No final point will bring lasting happiness. The source of our happiness and unhappiness cannot be any accomplished goal, acquired object, or any specific event. Our happiness is not stored somewhere in the future (just as our unhappiness is not caused by something that happened in the past). The true source of happiness (or unhappiness) lies in our current state of consciousness and in how we perceive the present moment.

Suffering occurs precisely when we leave the reality of the present moment and create a story in our heads about the future (or past). Constant desire for what comes next pulls us into a cycle of endless expectation, because of which the present moment loses its value. Inability to live fully in the present results in many of us experiencing our days in a state of perpetual dissatisfaction and incompleteness. We have endless desires and expectations that can never be fulfilled.

A person always wants more. They always want what is to come next. Life becomes a series of unfulfilled expectations where true happiness is always waiting around the corner. This does not apply only to long-term goals; this approach can be observed in the simplest everyday activities. A person at an ordinary level of consciousness cannot even savor the food they are eating because they are focused on anticipating the next course. Instead of savoring the bite of food they are eating, they think about dessert or the activity they will do next. They cannot savor any moment they are in because their mind is focused on the moment that will come after.

Conversely, when we live in the present moment, our desire is constantly fulfilled. We savor every bite of food and feel satisfied because our attention is focused on the current experience. This applies not only to food but also to sports activities, work matters, or sexual experiences. When we fully immerse ourselves in what we are doing, we achieve a state of maximum fulfillment and maximum satisfaction in every moment. This approach to life not only allows us to experience each moment more intensely but also uncover deeper layers of joy and satisfaction hidden in everyday activities.

Living in the present allows us to savor each moment, without constantly seeking something more or something that is to come later.

The true source of happiness and joy comes from realizing our own existence in the present moment. True happiness comes from how we view life in this moment. It is the only moment we can subjectively experience. Moment by moment.

Imagine you are playing a game that you really enjoy. If you constantly think about what you will do later or what happened a moment ago, you will not be able to fully enjoy the game, play it fully, or succeed at it.

That game is called life.
