From an early age, we were taught to believe that we must constantly manage life — meticulously control everything, think each matter through carefully, plan wisely, and make the right decisions. We were told that if we fail to watch over every detail, things will easily fall apart. But what if we were to discover that this notion of steering our lives is nothing more than a grand illusion, rooted in the evolutionary remnants of our minds?

Many people still believe that with enough information and careful planning, they could predict and control the course of their lives — or even the world around them. Of course, this is impossible. Even if we could hypothetically know the position and state of every atom in the universe (which is both practically and theoretically impossible), we still could not predict the future, nor control or command the flow of life. Life is neither linear nor predictable. It follows no fixed script or plan that someone directs or that we could decipher. All events unfold spontaneously and naturally, as the outcome of countless interconnected causes.

Findings from quantum physics show us that at the most fundamental level, events do not occur with precision and predictability (deterministically), but only with a certain probability. Nothing is fixed, immutable, linearly arranged, and certainly not predetermined. There is no way to determine an outcome with absolute certainty. We can only estimate its probability.

To this we may add chaos theory, which reveals that even in seemingly predictable systems, minute changes in initial conditions can produce dramatically different outcomes. This phenomenon is known as the butterfly effect — a metaphor suggesting that the flap of a butterfly's wings in one corner of the world may, over time, contribute to the formation of a hurricane on the opposite side of the planet. Today we know that even the smallest influence, one we would ordinarily ignore, can lead to an entirely different result in the long-term evolution of a system than we originally anticipated.

We simply cannot fully comprehend, control, or predict the world. Not because we lack intelligence, but because the very nature of reality is nonlinear and unpredictable. Yet it is also open and creative.

No matter how hard we try to deceive ourselves, there comes a moment when we must finally acknowledge that everything happens on its own — and that our efforts to control it were always nothing more than a mistaken and naive notion arising from the limitations of our minds.

An individual in a developed state of consciousness knows well that many aspects of life operate independently of our personal interventions, emotional and psychological reactions, or subjective opinions. There exists a higher intelligence and broader natural processes that govern these events without our influence.

The sun rises and sets, flowers bloom and leaves fall always at exactly the right time. Hair grows without our effort, oxygen flows into our lungs without conscious control, blood courses through our veins without us commanding it. Digestion takes place on its own, the body absorbs nutrients, cells renew and wounds heal without our conscious effort. Planets orbit the sun, water flows from the mountains toward the sea, and wind blows without anyone directing it. The seasons change with regularity, the ocean tides work like clockwork, rain arrives when the time is right, and seeds grow into plants when conditions are favourable. And yet, we believe it is we who run life.

Nature cleanses itself, heals, and restores balance without us reminding it how. It knew how to do this long before we humans appeared on this planet.

The human body, too, is part of this innate wisdom. Our bodies know how to heal, how to fight infections, how to regenerate. What is it that we actually control?

Even our thoughts arise spontaneously, without us consciously creating or planning them. Thoughts are expressions of deeper layers of our psyche — the subconscious, collective influences, and above all, the state of our consciousness. We are not the creators of our thoughts, nor do we control when or which thought appears — we can merely notice it arriving and departing.

Every moment is a natural continuation of the one before it, and everything that exists is part of a perfectly harmonised whole. Life does not need our control or constant intervention. Quite the opposite — the more we trust it and allow it to flow freely, the more harmoniously it unfolds.

The same intelligence that moves the tides and makes trees grow also moves our lives. The human mind, however, insists that it is the one directing our life — or even the lives of others. It ceaselessly tries to control, command, plan, analyse, categorise, judge, evaluate, organise, compare, predict, and manage. Let us pause for a moment and reflect. Why do we try so hard to run everything? And what exactly is it that we wish to control?

The truth is that life flows entirely without effort. The best thing we can do is not stand in its way. This is also the true meaning of surrender — to accept life in all its forms and expressions, to not obstruct anything natural, to fight nothing, to force nothing, simply to allow life to flow and be what it is.

To surrender to a higher intelligence and to acknowledge it as the Source of our own existence and our own life means to accept it and to connect with it. The moment we take this step is the moment of union with the Source and the All-Pervading Principle representing the highest degree of intelligence. When this moment arrives, it will feel like the most natural thing in the world.

Everything that has happened in our lives up to this point will suddenly make perfect sense.

When we speak of the All-Pervading Principle or the Source, we do not mean the biological principles that govern living beings, nor the physical forces that created planets, stars, galaxies, or even entire universes. We point to an unlimited, impersonal, and all-encompassing Source from which not only the universe itself originates, but absolutely everything — both what we can and cannot imagine.

A person at an ordinary level of consciousness believes they are the one directing their life, the one who acts, who commands the course of events. They live in the conviction that everything depends on them — on their decisions, their plans, their abilities and efforts. In truth, however, they were never the one steering the course of life — they merely believed in this illusion.

Let us ask ourselves a simple question: Did we decide the exact sequence of events that brought us to this moment, or did things simply happen, moment by moment? Did we choose our thoughts before they appeared? No. The mind rebels against this, because it wants to feel that it is the one in control. The truth, however, is that it never was.

The revelation that the control we took for granted is actually nothing but an illusion can be unsettling at first — and for some people, even frightening.

One quietly tells oneself: "I have to plan everything carefully so it turns out well," or "If I don't keep them in check, everything will fall apart," or "I need to have clearly defined goals, otherwise I'll lose my way." But these are thoughts born of fear, and they fail to account for the complexity of reality, which overlaps in countless layers of possibility.

If we were to stop for a moment and carefully examine what we can actually control, manage, and plan, we would quickly discover that it is very little. Even many decisions we consider our own are not conscious. They are often merely automatic responses to circumstances, subconscious programmes, or learned behavioural patterns. Life situations unfold without us being able to command them to the extent we imagine.

To believe that we truly direct the events in our lives is like standing in the middle of the ocean, waving our arms, and believing we control the direction of the waves. Such a notion is absurd. We know that in reality, every single wave is shaped by thousands of invisible forces — the gravity of the Moon and the Sun, winds arriving from vast distances, ocean currents, water temperature, the depth of the ocean, and many other factors. In exactly the same way, every decision we make has arisen from countless events, influences, and circumstances.

For example, we believe that we chose a particular job on our own. We feel this decision was ours. In reality, however, it was shaped by innumerable factors — the family we grew up in, the education we received, the teachers we encountered, the friends who inspired us, the films and books we watched and read, the economic situation in the country, our innate abilities, and also seemingly random encounters that subconsciously steered us in a certain direction. Our apparently free decision is the consequence of thousands of small events and circumstances that we never had under control — and most of which we never even noticed.

In reality, a human being is more of an observer than an active agent. More one who watches the current of life than one who leads it. We sail upon the ocean of life, either harnessing its force for our benefit or fighting against it senselessly. The more deeply we realise that life flows even without our control, the more we free ourselves from the need to control, command, plan, and attempt to manage every aspect of our existence.

True inner peace lies not in control, but in releasing the need to control. In surrendering to life, we discover extraordinary freedom. At last, we no longer need to comment on everything, hold an opinion about everything, or try to run everything. The moment we begin to trust that everything happening carries a deeper meaning, our life transforms. Paradoxically, when we relinquish control, life begins to flow far more harmoniously and effortlessly. The struggle ceases and everything aligns of its own accord. Suddenly, things begin to work out in ways we would not have dared to dream. Moments we might call miracles become entirely commonplace.

Our task was never to run the universe — we were merely standing in its way.

When we surrender control to a higher intelligence, it does not mean passivity or resignation — it means opening ourselves to a broader spectrum of possibilities than our mind could ever devise or even take into consideration.

We are not trying to manage every detail or cling to a specific outcome — instead, we trust that life will bring what is in deeper alignment with our true potential.

If we fixate on a particular goal, we restrict the creative force of life to a very narrow field of possibilities.

For instance, if we fix our minds on earning an exact sum — say, a million dollars — and moreover insist that it must happen through a specific channel, be it a single project, a single investment, or a single career path, we drastically narrow our field of possibilities. The mind latches onto one scenario and begins to dismiss or label everything else as "wrong."

In practice, this means we may overlook opportunities that would be more natural for us, less stressful, more meaningful, or enriching in ways that go beyond the merely financial.

By surrendering control over the precise form of the outcome, we do not invite chaos — on the contrary, we open ourselves to a broader spectrum of possibilities.

The result can be a reality that not only surpasses our original expectations, but arrives more naturally, with less resistance, without unnecessary stress, and in far greater harmony with life as a whole.

Many people confuse surrendering to the universe with attempting to steer it. They claim to trust a higher intelligence, yet in truth they have a precise plan they wish to see fulfilled: "I want to marry this partner, have two children — a girl and a boy — stay in this job until October of next year, and ideally, my mother-in-law would move to a different city." Others pray, yet in reality they are issuing orders about what should happen, what must not happen, who should come, and who should leave.

Of course, this is not surrender — it is an attempt to manipulate the highest intelligence for personal gain. Surrender does not mean using the supreme intelligence as a tool for fulfilling our egotistic fantasies. It means releasing the need for control, trusting the wisdom of life, opening ourselves to all possibilities, and allowing ourselves to be guided — not issuing commands.

To be precise, what we surrender is illusory control — over things we never actually controlled, managed, or commanded in the first place. It does not mean inaction. It does not mean lying down and waiting for miracles. It does not mean ignoring the material world or trying to violate the principles of reality. On the contrary, it means understanding those principles more deeply. It means recognising that the process of reality's creation unfolds through us — not outside of us, and not without us.

Although the phrase "surrender" might mistakenly suggest an escape from action into inaction, it is rather a transition from trying to manipulate reality to consciously collaborating with it.

When we stop trying to manage and control everything, we begin to perceive the subtle impulses through which the universe naturally guides us. And it is precisely then that a far broader potential of possibilities opens up — one that is aligned with the deepest intelligence of life itself.
