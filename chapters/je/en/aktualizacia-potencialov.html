Every moment in life represents a point at which a countless number of different possibilities exist. Reality is not fixed — it is a continuously evolving field of probabilities, profoundly influenced by our level of consciousness, our thoughts, attitudes, and true motives. What we think about ourselves, about the world around us, and about other people directly influences which of these innumerable possibilities actualises as fact — and thus manifests in the material world.

When our consciousness is filled with fear, guilt, self-pity, shame, or anger, we increase the probability of those possibilities that reflect these emotions. The emotions that dominate our lives shape our attitudes, our thoughts, and our decisions.

Imagine a person who has been living for a long time in an emotional state of guilt. In the past, they made certain mistakes which they are unable to process constructively and maturely. Their subconscious — and often conscious — thoughts are destructive: "I'm not good enough to deserve success," or "I deserve to be punished for my past mistakes," or "I truly don't deserve this."

These thoughts become a filter through which they perceive the world, and they unconsciously make various life decisions based on these deeper layers of the psyche.

For example, when a new opportunity appears — a better job, a relationship, a chance to grow — they subconsciously reject or sabotage it, because their inner programme of guilt whispers that they "don't deserve" it. Somewhere in the background, there is a fear of retribution for past mistakes. They are afraid that the higher they rise, the harder they will fall. And so they live in frustration, exhaustion, and continually reinforce the belief that life is a struggle.

•••

The fundamental problem is that a person in a low state of consciousness typically does not recognise their thoughts as something separate from themselves. They are fully identified with them — they believe that their thoughts are a truthful picture of reality, rather than a transient state of consciousness that can be changed.

In thoughts of fear, the mind tells us: "If I mess this up, everything will collapse." We take this as objective fact, not as a thought born of fear. In guilt, we tell ourselves: "I'm not good enough." We experience this as a truth about who we are, not as a programme or a mental pattern. When anger and frustration prevail, it colours our view of the world: "That person deliberately insulted me." The individual does not see that this is merely their subjective interpretation of the situation, and that reality may be entirely different.

In higher states of consciousness, thoughts rooted in fear, guilt, shame, self-pity, or anger naturally cease to arise. As the level of consciousness grows, such thoughts lose the ground beneath them, because we understand their origin, their evolutionary purpose, and their influence gradually dissolves. The result is that reality is perceived and experienced in an entirely different way — as more harmonious, more creative, more fluid, and filled with a deep trust in life.

When we realise that the reality around us is not fixed, but is a living field of possibilities that is constantly shaped by our consciousness, we feel the true power and influence we have over our lives.

We are not merely biological organisms struggling to survive in a chaotic world full of chance. We are co-creators of the reality we live every day — conscious beings who can participate in building their own story in alignment with the flow of life itself.

•••

In order to properly understand this dynamic and not remain at the level of motivational platitudes, let us try to shed a little light on the process.

Reality exists at every moment as a field of infinite possibilities, waiting to be actualised — through conscious observation, thinking, decision-making, and ultimately action — into a concrete manifestation in spacetime, in the physical world we are able to perceive.

What we perceive as reality is an actualisation of potentials whose probability of arising is shaped primarily by our conscious and subconscious influence.

We do not control this process in the sense of directing every variable according to our own ideas. It is more that through consciousness, we increase the probability of a reality that is aligned with our perception of the world, our intention, our thoughts, attitudes, and our true motives. It must also be in harmony with the deeper essence of life and its natural flow.

As we explained in the previous chapter, surrendering control is not an act of passivity — it is a crucial variable in this entire mechanism, because it allows consciousness to release itself from the limited conceptions of the ego and to open up to a wider spectrum of possibilities. Rather than forcing a particular outcome, it creates space for more optimal, more harmonious, and often unexpectedly better solutions that we could never have foreseen through our own planning.

Let us take a simplified look at this entire mechanism, so that we can better understand how our state of consciousness, our true motives, and the degree of openness in the process of surrender influence the kind of experience we ultimately have in the physical world.
